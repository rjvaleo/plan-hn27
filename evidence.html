Where OpenAI already leads

  • A rollout guide that starts with owners.Step 1 of the admin rollout guide assigns an owner to each part of the rollout before anything is configured. A separate quickstart gives a nine-item checklist. Rollout guide, Quickstart
  • A written method for measuring value, with rules for reading the numbers.Agree the result with the business owner and record the baseline. A zero may be a reporting gap, a sample is not a total, and estimates stay labeled as estimates. Usage Insights
  • A published delivery sequence.For managed deployments: outcomes and success criteria, integration, security review, acceptance testing, a staged rollout, then review. Help Center
  • Forward-deployed engineering, explained in public.The head of the team has written how it works: start with the outcome, then make success repeatable. The posting’s last duty asks for the same thing. October 22, 2026
  • Six months’ notice before a generally available model retires.Longer than the 60 days Anthropic and Azure state, with the shutdown date readable from the models endpoint. Deprecations
  • A partner network with a public list.73 partners named on the page, three tiers, and a pilot that aligns partner staff with OpenAI’s own forward-deployed teams. Partner Network
  • Uptime in public.The status page shows uptime per component: 99.97% for the APIs from August to November 2026. Status

What a delivery lead should know about this market

Four things that shape a plan, whichever provider it is built on.

  • Every large provider now names forward-deployed engineers.OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Amazon all describe them on public pages, and Anthropic funds training for them. The engineers are no longer the difference. How their work is planned and joined to the customer’s is.
  • The model can retire during the program.For generally available models, stated notice runs from 45 days to six months across the providers I read. A launch date and a retirement date belong on the same page.
  • Measurement has moved from seats to outcomes.OpenAI, Google and Microsoft all publish ways to connect usage to results. Of the rollout guides I read, only Google’s makes a baseline survey a named step.
  • Capacity is bought ahead.OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Amazon all document reserved capacity per model. It has to be sized before launch, from a test and not a guess.

What is not on this page

While reading, I found seven places where OpenAI’s public pages disagree with each other, and a few smaller inconsistencies. Most touch things a customer plans around: capacity, retirement dates and reporting.

Those are in a findings report I will send to OpenAI directly. A public page is the wrong place for them.
